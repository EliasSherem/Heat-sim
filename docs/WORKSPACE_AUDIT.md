# Imported-job thermal connection audit

This audit concerns the production imported-job model used by the main simulation workspace. It distinguishes numerical implementation checks from experimental validation. No printer, controller or vendor program is executed. The original job is treated as data.

## Available real evidence

The available packaged job is `Dachshund_lowpoly_15mm_tall_proportional.mjob`, with source SHA-256 `5cd20985e62718377dce8b5944725853820ec8806e5d7f398a559a21baf8b136`. Its retained source copy is under `results/imports/5cd20985e62718377dce/source.mjob`. Importing that source again checks the packaged layer/source hashes; its bytes remain unchanged. It contains 725 source layers and 1,404,024 nonzero exposure vectors. Bounds from the imported exposure coordinates span approximately 8.076 × 30.156 × 21.750 mm. These are job-coordinate bounds, not an independently measured part envelope.

The source's first 100 layers contain only section 16, labelled support in this package. Body lower-infill and lower-contour exposures begin at layer 100; infill at 101; upper contour at 102; upper infill at 103; contour at 105. A three-layer preview therefore demonstrates an early support-like region, not the main body. A continuous representative short range is source layers **0–109**, containing 428,955 vectors and all seven used sections: 0, 1, 2, 6, 7, 8 and 16. Its imported laser-on duration is 16.2420 seconds, before jumps and the user's estimated 3-second inter-layer pause. Starting at layer 200 is quicker for some studies but requires an explicit uniform-preheat earlier-build assumption and omits the earlier thermal history.

The available legacy `17-4 Test.htmjob` is separately retained with SHA-256 `e6b09ca8d7a16ce5660005537dfe6213dbc1471836fa07d960ca81c9153daa19`. Its disabled core recipe is declared as 380 W / 888 mm/s, but no core exposure appears in the decoded vectors. Reserved section 9 points are recorded diagnostically and omitted. Test fixtures additionally contain disabled-core records and prove those records are discarded.

## Numerical evidence

`tests/test_physics_connection.py` includes real-package immutability/import checks, real mixed-region vector/order/timing checks, actual legacy disabled-region checks, a persistent-heat/no-new-heat experiment, powder/future-cell conductance checks, independent four-alloy solves and rendered-value-to-NPZ comparisons. Existing checks in `test_build_solver.py`, `test_run_planning.py`, `test_job_import.py`, `test_materials.py`, `test_solver.py` and `test_verification.py` cover known-solution conduction, source-energy normalization, fusion enthalpy/inversion, activation, cooling, timing truncation and global energy bookkeeping. Viewer and workspace tests cover source/frame identities and physical-time selection separately from the physics tests.

The zero-new-heat experiment closes its complete energy ledger; its frame values retain first-layer heat after the second activation and show plate heating during an off period. The tests do not equate an attractive animation or a passing energy check with predictive agreement with the printer.

## Independently inspected new-workspace calculation

`results/builds/20261116-105810-c55151` contains two complete independent calculations of the actual Dachshund source, in 17-4PH and 316L. Both start at layer 0 and end after layer 109, with no warm-start, time cap, vector cap or cropped domain. This is a continuous 110-layer prefix of the 725-layer source, including all seven used regions and 428,955 vectors. It is not a complete 725-layer simulation.

Each case saved 80 fields from 0 to 398.9949665865 physical seconds, ending at source layer 109. Independent source arithmetic gives 16.2420420776 seconds of marking, 55.2529245090 seconds of assumed jump motion/settling, 327 seconds of the user-estimated inter-layer pause and 0.5 seconds of cooldown. Both cases have the same stored exposure-event schedule, independent material tables, and distinct final temperature fields (maximum cellwise difference 7.3309021 K). Their coarse 6 × 17 × 5 grids use 2 mm lateral / 1.5 mm vertical cells and 100 ms exposure grouping; this check does not resolve a melt pool or establish convergence.

The saved source copies match the original imported SHA. Every stored scan endpoint, sequence index, section, power, speed and duration was compared with the imported vectors. The two energy ledgers close with relative errors of 2.4813×10⁻¹⁴ and 2.7344×10⁻¹⁴. All emitted viewport cell temperatures were independently compared with the raw saved float32 fields converted from Kelvin to Celsius, without applying two-decimal rounding. The raw source hash and material properties, finite frame values and current-input matching were checked independently of the report's success label. Altering power, speed, beam or primary absorptivity correctly causes this saved comparison to fail the configuration-match check.

The separate attempt `results/builds/20261116-105618-ba2971` has a matching run-specific cancellation marker and worker acknowledgement. It retained 67 published frames through 333.3375670217 seconds, latest source layer 91, with no completed case. Those are partial fields rather than a finished prediction. The complete numeric record is `examples/workspace-verification.json`. Browser interaction and screenshot observations are recorded separately from these saved-artifact checks.

The complete saved run `results/builds/20261116-112628-d360db` also verifies the corrected playback geometry. All **1,404,024 emitting scans across 725 layers** are represented by **545,291 connected display spans**, including **325,019 support spans** representing all 1,177,315 support scans. The previous roughly 12,000-path display sample omitted intervening connected geometry even though the final field covered the full print. The corrected display has no global path-count cutoff: it simplifies only connected emitting scans within the same layer and section, preserving jumps, disabled-event boundaries, isolated paths and original endpoints. Each merged span is limited to 25 ms; the longest merged span in this job is 6.596 ms.

An independent distance check covered all **2,808,048 original scan endpoints**, finding a maximum deviation of **0.02 mm** from their retained display spans. This is a display-curve tolerance, not thermal resolution. The neutral preview likewise represents all vectors and layers, with 545,071 spans. Frozen source, timeline, configuration, manifest and all 80 stored thermal fields remain unchanged by geometry preparation. `docs/playback-geometry-verification.json` records the source hash, counts, independent endpoint-error check and saved-artifact checks; it makes no browser-verification claim. Large geometry uses content-addressed assets served by the existing local application.

## Remaining model limits

- Whole-build calculations resolve coarse thermal accumulation and cooling. They do not resolve microscopic melt-pool width/depth, surface tension, melt flow, recoil, vaporisation, keyhole instability, spatter, porosity or a qualified print-quality optimum.
- Beam coupling, delivered power, physical beam diameter/profile, powder behaviour and timing need measurement. One alloy's fitted coupling cannot identify another alloy's absorptivity.
- Source paths give an exposure footprint, not necessarily watertight CAD. Optional aligned STL is declared supplementary geometry. Support-like process labels and fine contacts should not be treated as experimentally identified geometry.
- Representative checks have shown mesh, event-grouping and plate-depth sensitivity. Saved peaks may be missed by sparse frame sampling; computed per-integration-step peak metrics and stored-frame samples must remain distinct.
- A refined cropped whole-build region is still the same thermal model and omits surrounding heat transfer. The specialist single-track laboratory is a distinct numerical configuration; neither is validated microscopic melt-flow prediction.
- Synchronised material comparison means independent solves shown at the same physical time and shared temperature scale. It does not mean matching frame indices, forcing equal temperature histories, or changing simulation physics with the playback-speed control.
